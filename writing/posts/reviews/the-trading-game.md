# Overview

```ad-quote
The best job a concert pianist can get nowadays is trader for Citibank. It pays very well
```

This is an okay book. It's leftfield enough to escape the centrist dad category.

The plot itself isn't groundbreaking: Gary is good at maths, Gary is poor, Gary wants money, Gary becomes a trader. He wins a card-based trading game at the LSE, the game gets him an internship at Citibank, the internship gets him a seat on the short-term interest rate desk, and the desk makes him a millionaire in his mid-twenties.

I can't say I learned loads about trading, but it was nice to see class drawn so clearly. Gary describes getting into a good university, working hard, and then discovering a world of internships, interviews and suits that he had never been let in on.

```ad-quote
I'd believed it when the teachers back at school had told me: study hard and do well in your exams and you will get a good job. I had been an idiot. I had been a fool.
```

The book obviously takes inspiration from Liar's Poker. A young man lands at a big, bad bank, finds a trading floor full of gluttonous characters like "the Slug", and leaves to write about it. Like Liar's Poker, it is part memoir. Stevenson takes the time to delve into his own interests and hyperfixations alongside his deteriorating mental health. His 5k times get a mention, as does a tour of Tokyo's cuisine.

Where the two books part ways is in how the narrator treats himself. Stevenson dwells on the moral failings of everyone around him while building up excuses and empathy for himself. I think that comes from the author also being a character in his own story: the narrative reads as one person's account rather than an assertion of fact, a perspective rather than a record.

There have been factual claims made against the book, specifically disputing whether Stevenson really was the most profitable trader at Citi. I don't think it matters much if he's lying. If anything, it makes the book more engaging to think he could be, given how well that ties into the unreliable narrator and that sense of perspective over fact. Even if part of it is fiction, fiction is still good at teaching a lesson.
